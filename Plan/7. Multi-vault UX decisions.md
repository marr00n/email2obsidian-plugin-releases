**Multiple Vault Support — User Experience Decisions**
Grill session, 2026-09-09

A second design pass over multi-vault support, this time driven by user
experience rather than mechanism. The mechanism was settled in
`Plan/6. Multiple vault support.md` on 2026-09-07 and most of it stands. What
changed is the shape of the control the user actually touches.

**This document supersedes section 7 of Plan/6 and the control shape in
`docs/adr/0001-per-install-receive-policy.md`.** The *policy* those documents
describe — each install decides for itself, filtering happens locally, unmarked
email is a first-class case — is unchanged and still correct. Only the UI that
expresses it is replaced.

Terminology follows `CONTEXT.md`: a **Vault Marker** is the routing name a
sender puts on an email (`@@Work`); a **Hashtag** is a keyword that becomes an
Obsidian tag; an **Unmarked Email** carries no marker.

## The settings control

Two controls replace the prior three-way radio group.

```
Vault markers                                          [Pro]

Markers          [ Work, Second Brain                       ]
                 Comma-separated. Leave blank to receive every marker.
                 Each vault is set up separately. A vault left blank
                 receives everything — including mail marked for your
                 other vaults.
                 Last fetch declined: Art (4), Personal (12)

Unmarked emails                                          [ON]
                 Emails sent without a marker.
```

All copy above is placeholder. Final wording is the maintainer's to write; the
Pro label in particular needs rewording, and so does the plan warning below.

### Why free text and not a list

A checklist seeded from the server's `vaults` facet was designed and rejected.
The facet is derived from live email, and the service deletes email after 72
hours, so it only ever shows markers used in the last three days. A user with
three vaults who has only emailed one of them this week would see one row. The
other two vaults exist; the server simply cannot know about them. A control
whose contents silently omit two thirds of the answer is worse than a plain
text field, because it looks authoritative.

Facet-driven autocomplete was also considered and rejected: it carries the same
recency blind spot in a softer form, and adds a dependency on a response the
settings tab would otherwise not need.

**Consequence, accepted knowingly:** a typo is silent. `@@Wrok` routes nowhere,
produces no error, and after 72 hours the email is gone. Section "Declines are
visible" below is the mitigation.

### What a blank field means

**Blank means every marker.** It is not an empty allow list; it is the absence
of a filter.

This is what makes the release safe. The default state is blank markers plus
unmarked ON, which is byte-for-byte the behaviour of every install shipping
today. Nobody's sync empties on upgrade, no migration runs, and no first-run
prompt interrupts the majority of users who have one vault and no interest in
this feature.

It also removes a dead state. An earlier draft kept an "Any other marker"
wildcard toggle alongside the field, which meant that while the wildcard was on
— the necessary default — typing a marker did nothing observable. Folding
"everything" into the blank state removes the toggle and the dead state with
it.

**Accepted limitation:** there is an allow list but no deny list. A vault
cannot say "everything except Art". A user wanting that must list what they do
want.

### Unmarked email is a separate question

Whether a vault takes marked mail and whether it takes unmarked mail are
orthogonal, so they are two controls rather than three combined radio options.
Default ON, for the same upgrade-safety reason.

## Behaviour

### Matching

Markers are compared case-insensitively after trimming, per `CONTEXT.md`.
`Work` and `work` name the same Obsidian Vault. The field is parsed on comma
boundaries — whitespace cannot be the separator because markers may legally
contain spaces (`Second Brain`), and commas are not legal inside a marker, so
they are unambiguous. Entries are trimmed and duplicates collapsed
case-insensitively.

### Changing the markers is retroactive, and announced before it happens

Ticking a marker back on, or adding one, releases mail that was previously
declined. Up to 72 hours of it may still be on the server.

**On edit, the settings tab states what will arrive, and offers to fetch it
now:** "Art: 7 emails from the last 3 days will arrive on the next fetch"
with a Fetch now button.

The count comes from the plugin's own fetch log, not from the server. Declines
have to be recorded there anyway — `paginateEmails` stops at the first
already-seen id (`src/pipeline.ts:252`) on the assumption that the log is a
contiguous run of the newest email, and locally-filtered declines would make it
sparse and break that assumption. Given a decline entry stores its marker and
its timestamp, the count is a local lookup: zero requests, instant, available
offline.

The forward-only alternative was rejected. A user who mistypes a marker at
setup and corrects it ten minutes later would otherwise lose that mail
permanently, and "Fetch all notes" is not a safe recovery route while it
duplicates rather than reconciles (defect 5 in Plan/6).

### Declines are visible

This is the safety net for the silent-typo risk taken above, and it is
diagnostic rather than suggestive — it reports what happened, it never proposes
a marker.

- The sync notice gains one counter: `12 added, 3 skipped, 5 not for this vault`.
- The settings tab shows a passive read-only line beneath the markers field:
  `Last fetch declined: Art (4), Wrok (1)`.

A typo appears there by name the first time mail carrying it is turned away. So
does an unconfigured sibling vault: seeing `declined: Personal (12)` is how a
user learns that Personal exists in their stream and is presumably still
taking everything.

### Notes are stamped, uniformly

Every imported note carries `email2obsidianVault` in frontmatter alongside the
existing `email2obsidianID`, written as an empty string for unmarked email
rather than omitted. A query never has to handle a missing key.

```yaml
---
email2obsidianID: 42
email2obsidianVault: Art
---
```

This makes a note self-describing about why it landed in this vault, gives
Bases and Dataview something to group on, and means any future re-route or
cleanup feature can work from local files. That last point matters more than it
looks: with a 72-hour retention window, a note older than three days cannot be
re-interrogated from the server at all, so anything not stamped at write time
is unrecoverable.

### Notes already in the vault are never touched

Narrowing the markers moves nothing, deletes nothing, and re-stamps nothing.
Files on disk by then are the user's — edited, linked, embedded in other notes
— and a tidiness win does not justify that risk. This confirms the decision in
Plan/6 section 8.

## The Starter-plan trap

Vault routing is gated at ingest, not at read (`docs/server-api-contract.md`).
On Starter without a grant, `vault` is always null **and** the `@@Work` token
is left in the subject text. That user sees notes titled `@@Work Weekly
review`, a markers field that receives nothing however they fill it in, and — if
they ever switch unmarked OFF — a vault that receives nothing at all. The
reasonable conclusion is that the plugin is broken.

**Decision: warn in settings; leave the note title alone.**

The signature is cheap to detect and cannot occur on an entitled account: a
summary whose `vault` is null and whose subject begins with `@@`. Once seen,
the markers section shows an informational notice pointing at the user's plan.
It never blocks anything.

Stripping the `@@` token from the filename was rejected on two grounds. It
crosses the line `docs/adr/0001` drew — the plugin would be parsing a marker
the server deliberately withheld — and it deletes the visible evidence that
makes the warning credible.

**The plugin cannot know the user's plan and must not try.** The contract is
explicit that no route branches on plan, and the ingest-only gate means a
downgraded account still returns non-null `vault` values on older rows. So the
markers section is *labelled* Pro but always remains editable — never disabled,
never hidden. A lapsed-pro user with months of marked mail still needs the
control that filters it.

## Carried over unchanged from Plan/6

- A Vault Marker names a whole Obsidian Vault, never a folder within one.
  Notes continue to land in `notesFolder`.
- Filtering happens locally. `?vault=` is deliberately not used on the sync
  path, because the same email is a decline in one vault and an accept in
  another, and the filter cannot express "unmarked" at all.
- The plugin never falls back to parsing `@@` out of the subject to route.
  Unmarked means `vault === null`, full stop.
- A plugin instance can neither see nor name any other Obsidian Vault on the
  machine.

## Changed from Plan/6

| Plan/6 | Now |
|---|---|
| Three-way radio: All / Only marked X / Marked X + unmarked | Free-text markers field + unmarked toggle |
| One marker per install (section 7) | Any number, comma-separated |
| Marker chosen from a dropdown seeded by the `vaults` facet | No facet dependency in settings at all |
| Prefill from `app.vault.getName()` | No prefill — vault name and marker often differ |
| `email2obsidianVault` omitted when unmarked | Always written, empty when unmarked |
| Policy change silently reconsiders declined mail | Count shown at the moment of editing, with Fetch now |
| — | Starter-plan detection and warning |
| — | Overlap warning: a blank vault takes everyone's mail |

## Implementation surface

Updates the list in Plan/6.

- `src/api.ts` — add `vault: string | null` to `EmailSummary` and
  `EmailDetail`. Do **not** add a `vault` param to `EmailListRequest`. The
  `vaults` facet on `EmailListResponse` is no longer needed by settings; add it
  only if something else wants it.
- `src/main.ts` — `markers: string[]` and `receiveUnmarked: boolean` on
  `Email2ObsidianSettings`, defaulting to `[]` and `true`. Comma parsing,
  trimming and case-insensitive dedupe in `normalizeSettings`. The two
  controls, the decline readout, the pending-backfill line with its Fetch now
  button, the overlap warning, the Pro label, the plan warning.
- `src/pipeline.ts` — decide each summary before the `getEmail` call
  (`src/pipeline.ts:99`), so a decline skips the body and every attachment
  download. Declined counter into the notice (`src/pipeline.ts:207`). Declined
  ids into the log. Drop the relevant declined entries when markers change.
- `src/fetch-log-store.ts` — a declined entry needs its marker and timestamp,
  so the backfill count can be computed locally and bounded to the 72-hour
  window.
- `src/helpers.ts` — `email2obsidianVault` in `renderEmailMarkdown`
  (`src/helpers.ts:154`).

New settings copy must be sentence case; `obsidianmd/ui/sentence-case` already
accounts for 5 of the 17 existing lint errors (defect 6 in Plan/6).

## Open

- **All user-facing copy** is placeholder and needs rewriting, particularly the
  Pro label and the plan warning.
- **Where the section sits** in the settings tab, and its heading, is undecided.
- **Whether the Fetch now button runs a full `fetch-new`** or a targeted pull of
  just the released ids.
- **The defects listed in Plan/6** (null hashtags, pagination loop, fetch-all
  duplication) are unaddressed and one of them — fetch-all duplication — is
  load-bearing for the recovery story here.
